# Why Marigold Seeds Deserve a Spot in Every Garden

Marigolds are often treated as an afterthought — a border flower, a festive decoration. That undersells them. Few plants do as much practical work in a garden for as little effort, which is why they're one of the most reliable seeds a grower can start with.

## Easy to Grow, Hard to Get Wrong

Marigold seeds germinate quickly, typically within 5-7 days in warm conditions, and tolerate a wide range of soil types without much fuss. They don't demand rich soil or precise watering schedules, which makes them a forgiving choice for beginners and a low-maintenance filler for experienced gardeners managing larger beds. Full sun and moderate watering are really all they ask for.

## A Working Plant, Not Just a Pretty One

**Pest deterrence.** Marigold roots release compounds that repel nematodes in the soil, and the strong scent of the foliage discourages aphids, whiteflies, and several other common pests above ground. This is why you'll often see marigolds planted along the borders of vegetable beds rather than off in a separate flower patch.

**Companion planting.** Interplanting marigolds with tomatoes, brinjal, chillies, and beans is a long-standing practice for exactly this reason — they act as a living pest barrier without any spraying involved.

**Pollinator support.** Bees and other pollinators are drawn to marigold blooms, which helps improve fruit set on nearby vegetables that depend on pollination.

**Continuous colour.** Once established, marigolds flower prolifically from a single sowing through most of the warm season, and deadheading spent blooms encourages even more.

## Growing Notes

- **Sow depth:** Just barely cover the seed — marigold germinates better with some light exposure.
- **Spacing:** 20-30 cm apart depending on variety (French marigolds stay compact; African marigolds grow taller and bushier).
- **Sunlight:** Full sun produces the strongest blooms; partial shade is tolerated but growth will be leggier.
- **Watering:** Moderate and consistent — established plants are relatively drought-tolerant, but young seedlings need regular moisture.
- **Bonus:** Save seed from spent flower heads at season's end — dry them fully before storing, and you'll have next season's marigolds for free.

## The Takeaway

If you're building out a vegetable garden and only have room in the budget or the beds for one flower, marigold is the practical choice — it looks good, asks for little, and quietly protects everything planted around it.
