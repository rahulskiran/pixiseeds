# The Complete Guide to Starting Vegetable Seeds Indoors

Starting vegetables from seed indoors gives you a head start on the growing season, control over soil quality from day one, and access to varieties you'll rarely find as nursery starts. It also costs a fraction of buying seedlings. Here's how to do it properly.

## Why Start Indoors at All

Direct-sowing works for fast, hardy crops like radish, beans, and carrots. But slower, warmth-loving vegetables — tomato, chilli, brinjal (eggplant), capsicum — need 6 to 10 weeks of controlled growth before they can handle outdoor conditions. Starting indoors protects young seedlings from erratic temperatures, birds, and damping-off caused by waterlogged garden soil, and lets you transplant only your strongest plants.

## What You'll Need

- **Seed trays or small pots** with drainage holes
- **A seed-starting mix** — lighter and more sterile than garden soil, which reduces fungal issues
- **A warm, bright spot** — a sunny windowsill works, but a spot that gets at least 6 hours of light is ideal
- **A spray bottle or fine watering can** for gentle, even moisture
- **Labels** — seedlings look identical long before they fruit

## Step-by-Step

**1. Check the seed packet timing.** Every variety lists a recommended sowing window relative to your last frost date or local season. Sowing too early gives you leggy, root-bound seedlings; too late and you lose growing days.

**2. Fill trays and sow at the right depth.** A general rule: plant a seed roughly twice as deep as it is wide. Fine seeds like tomato or chilli barely need covering — a light dusting of mix is enough.

**3. Keep the mix consistently moist, never soggy.** Cover trays with a clear lid or plastic wrap until germination to hold humidity, then remove it immediately once shoots appear to prevent mould.

**4. Watch the temperature.** Most vegetable seeds germinate best between 20-28°C. Below that, germination slows dramatically or stalls.

**5. Thin seedlings once true leaves appear.** The first pair of leaves you see are "seed leaves" — wait for the second, true set before thinning to the strongest one or two seedlings per cell.

**6. Harden off before transplanting.** Seven to ten days before moving seedlings outdoors, start exposing them to outside conditions for a few hours a day, increasing gradually. Skipping this step is the single most common reason indoor-started seedlings collapse after transplant.

## A Few Mistakes Worth Avoiding

- **Overwatering.** More seedlings are lost to soggy soil and damping-off than to underwatering.
- **Low light.** Without enough light, seedlings stretch upward searching for it and become weak and floppy — a sunny window is often not enough in peak winter; consider a supplemental grow light.
- **Transplanting too early.** Wait until the plant has a sturdy root system and at least two sets of true leaves.

## Ready to Start

A strong seed is the foundation everything else depends on — choose fresh, high-germination seeds from a source you trust, follow the timing on the packet, and resist the urge to rush the process. The extra few weeks of care indoors pays off in stronger, more productive plants once they hit the garden.
